# Safe-Middle Copy

## Definition

The dominant failure mode of SaaS homepage copy: writing a sentence the buyer cannot disagree with and the user cannot picture, in an attempt to serve both audiences at once. The result is text that excludes nobody and lands with nobody. Every word is a placeholder; every reviewer signs off; no one in the buying committee feels addressed.

Coined in [[your-homepage-is-written-for-nobody]] ([[brian-rieb]]).

## Why It Matters

The homepage is the highest-leverage piece of copy a company owns. When it fails, the failure is silent — visitors leave without identifying what the product does and the team rarely hears about it. The pattern is also self-reinforcing: every competitor's homepage looks the same, so the bar feels normal. It is not normal. It is the genre eating itself.

This is a go-to-market problem dressed in copy. You cannot fix it inside the copy review. It has to be fixed by picking a reader before the writing starts.

## How It Works

**Two readers, two kinds of evidence.**

- The **user** is the practitioner who will live in the product. They want to see the work — screenshots, specific verbs, the shape of a day with the tool in it.
- The **buyer** is the exec who signs the contract. They will never log in. They want the outcome, the risk, and the mechanism that makes the outcome believable.

Marketing imagines a single reader and hedges both. The result is a sentence that the buyer cannot disagree with (because it says nothing concrete) and the user cannot picture (because every noun is abstract). "Value-led" copy. It is neither.

**The approval cycle finishes the job.** Legal sands off a claim. Product asks you to soften a verb. The chief marketing officer wants it to sound a little more "platform." Each hedge is rational in isolation; the sum is a headline that asserts only that the company exists.

## The Test

Read your hero headline to someone outside your industry. If they cannot describe the product back to you after thirty seconds, you are in the safe middle.

A sharper test: could your competitor swap their logo onto your homepage and ship it? If yes, the page is written for nobody.

## Anatomy of a Safe-Middle Line

> "Empower your teams to do their best work."

- **Empower** — a feeling, not a mechanism.
- **Your teams** — which teams? Sales? Engineering? Legal?
- **Best work** — by whose definition, against what baseline?

Every word survives review because it commits to nothing. A finance leader can squint and imagine cost savings. A head of infrastructure can squint and imagine governance. A practitioner can squint and imagine a better Monday. All of them are squinting. None of them are nodding.

## The Fix: Pick a Reader, Show Both

You can write for the buyer without abandoning the user. The homepage is not a billboard — it is the first stop on a multi-stop trip.

1. **Hero for the buyer.** State what the product does in nouns and verbs the buyer recognizes: the outcome, and the mechanism that delivers it.
2. **Next section for the user.** Show the work. The specific motion a practitioner makes inside the product.
3. **Defend the specifics.** Some of them will narrow your audience. That is the point.

The buyer reads the top. The user scrolls. Both find their evidence on the same page, in the order they care about it.
