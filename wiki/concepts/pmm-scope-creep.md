# PMM Scope Creep

## Definition

The gradual expansion of PMM's functional charter beyond positioning, launches, and sales enablement into 8–12+ adjacent areas — pricing, analyst relations, AI strategy, customer advocacy, and more — driven by organizational need rather than intentional role design. The named failure mode is **Death by Adjacency**: PMMs get pulled into workflows they don't own strategically because their proximity to revenue-critical processes makes them the default filler for unclaimed work.

## Why It Matters

When PMM absorbs everything, it does nothing well. Strategic work — the synthesis and clarity that only PMM can provide — gets consistently deprioritized in favor of urgent execution. The role becomes a staffing solution for organizational ambiguity rather than a strategic function.

The 2024 [[product-marketing-alliance]] survey found PMMs commonly work across "8–12 distinct functional areas, with many reporting unclear ownership boundaries." The data confirms what most PMMs feel: the job description and the actual job have diverged.

## How It Happens

Three dynamics reinforce each other:

1. **PMMs attract delegation** — they excel at ambiguity and cross-functional work, so ambiguous work finds them
2. **Revenue proximity = default connective tissue** — sitting close to sales, product, and marketing means PMM gets tapped as the coordinator for everything touching those teams
3. **Influence vs. ownership confusion** — organizations correctly want PMM to have *strategic influence* across functions; they incorrectly turn that influence into *operational ownership*

## The Fix

**Define the charter explicitly.** Document three categories:
- What PMM owns (positioning, messaging, launch strategy, competitive intelligence)
- What PMM influences (roadmap, pricing, demand gen messaging)
- What PMM does not do (list it explicitly — the "does not do" list is often the most important)

**Measure strategic outcomes, not activity.** Win-rate influence, message adoption, competitive displacement. Not decks produced, requests fulfilled, or meetings attended.

**Be a market intelligence engine, not a slide factory.** The high-leverage PMM contribution is synthesis and clarity — turning market signal into strategic direction. Slide production is downstream of that, not a proxy for it.
