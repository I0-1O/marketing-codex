# Your Homepage Is Written for Nobody

> Full text: [[your-homepage-is-written-for-nobody]] (articles/)

## Synopsis

A diagnosis of why most SaaS homepages tell you nothing about the product. The failure is not laziness or bad writing — it is structural. The homepage has two readers (the **user** who will live in the product, and the **buyer** who signs the contract), and marketing keeps pretending it has one. Splitting the difference produces "safe-middle" copy that the buyer cannot disagree with and the user cannot picture. Then the approval cycle sands off the remaining specifics. Result: a headline that asserts only that the company exists.

Anonymized homepage banner used as the dissection target: *"Empower your teams to do their best work."* Every word a placeholder. A finance leader, a head of infrastructure, and a practitioner can each squint at it and project their own meaning — none of them are nodding.

The fix is not to pick one audience and abandon the other. It is to use the homepage as the first stop on a multi-stop trip: hero for the buyer (outcome + mechanism), next section for the user (the specific motion inside the product). Harder than the safe middle. Pays off when someone outside your industry can describe the product back to you in thirty seconds.

## Key Arguments

- Vague homepages are an accident, not a strategy — the page is trying to please two readers and lands with neither.
- The buyer and the user need different evidence; hedging both produces feelings instead of facts.
- Committee review is the second cause: every reviewer adds a hedge, until the headline only asserts the company exists.
- "A homepage that says something to someone beats a homepage that says nothing to everyone."
- If your homepage could be your competitor's homepage with the logo swapped, it is not written for anyone.
