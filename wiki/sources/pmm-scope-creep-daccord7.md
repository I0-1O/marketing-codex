# Product Marketing Has a Scope Creep Problem — and the Data Proves It

## Summary

PMM has become a corporate miscellaneous drawer. The role originated with a focused charter — positioning, launches, sales enablement — but has expanded to 8–12+ functional areas as organizations delegate ambiguous work to whoever handles ambiguity best. A 2024 [[product-marketing-alliance]] survey found widespread unclear ownership boundaries across PMM functions.

The article names the failure mode "Death by Adjacency": PMMs are pulled into adjacent workflows not because they own them strategically but because their proximity to revenue-critical processes makes them the default connector. Strategic work gets deprioritized in favor of urgent execution.

Three organizational dynamics drive it:
1. PMMs excel at ambiguity → they attract delegated work
2. PMM sits close to revenue → it becomes default connective tissue
3. Organizations conflate *strategic influence* (appropriate) with *operational ownership* (scope creep)

## Recommended Fixes

- **Define charter explicitly** — document what PMM owns, influences, and does not do
- **Prioritize insights over artifacts** — be a "market intelligence engine," not a "slide factory"
- **Measure strategic outcomes** — win-rate influence, message adoption, competitive displacement; not activity volume

## Key Term

**Death by Adjacency** — overload resulting from proximity to critical organizational workflows; distinguished from intentional role expansion.
